Class-01 7 ~ 8 hours

Semiconductor Plasma Process & Applications

From the physical principles behind plasma formation to how those principles drive CVD, Dry Etch, and PVD processes — explored through a hands-on virtual lab platform featuring a hydrogen atom orbital calculator and visual diagrams for the octet rule.

CCP ICP CVD Dry Etch PVD · RPS
  1. 01

    Fundamental Properties & Macroscopic Understanding (M1 ~ M3)

    The essence revealed through the Big Bang and high-temperature plasma, the quantum-mechanical history of atomic structure (excitation, ionization), and the principles of chemical bonding and radicals.

  2. 02

    Maxwell's Equations & Electromagnetics Fundamentals (M4)

    A three-dimensional unpacking of Maxwell's equations and Gauss's law for electric fields, with analysis of how electromagnetic waves form field distributions as they pass through the chamber.

  3. 03

    Deposition & Etching Mechanisms in CCP/ICP (M5)

    A deep dive into the differences between CCP and ICP coupling, Faraday induction, and how asymmetric electrode structures form DC bias and control vertical ion energy.

  4. 04

    Next-Generation Plasma Applications in Semiconductors

    Hands-on optimization of remote plasma systems (RPS) for metal deposition (PVD), cleaning, and photoresist ashing processes.

Class-02 7 ~ 8 hours

RF Power Transmission Theory for Plasma Formation

Electromagnetic (RF) transmission theory and hands-on matching-network optimization for stable plasma formation and precise control. Comes with a dedicated visual textbook featuring tools for Maxwell's four equations and an E-to-H mode hysteresis transition graph analyzer.

Standing Wave Impedance Matching Topologies FDC RF Generator
  1. 01

    Electromagnetic Transmission & Maxwell's Equations (M1)

    The grand unification of electricity and magnetism, the wave nature of light and electromagnetic waves, and the origins of wave energy transfer through standing-wave theory.

  2. 02

    Plasma Fundamentals (M2)

    Valence shell energy, collisional excitation and gas ionization mechanisms, and an integrated mathematical and geometric view of impedance phase shift inside the chamber.

  3. 03

    RF Matching & Power Supply Systems (M3)

    Understanding RLC resonant circuits, and the matching principle behind maximum power transfer to a 50Ω load through auto-tunable L-type matching network design.

  4. 04

    Field-Data-Based RF System Diagnostics

    Diagnosing and analyzing process equipment anomalies using RF generator architecture and FDC parameter trends (tune/load position, Vrms, etc.).

Teaching Method

Every Lecture Begins with
Four Core Principles

Nothing about the flow is left to chance. Four principles, distilled from 20+ years of teaching experience, run through the design and delivery of every module.

In Session

Interactive Lecture · Built on Visual Materials

01

Intuitive Visualization of Abstract Theory

We never approach complex quantum mechanics, electromagnetics, or wave theory through memorized formulas. PhET interactive simulations, documentaries like Cosmos and the physics of light, and Eugene Khutoryansky's 3D visualizations combine to give shape to microscopic physical phenomena in your mind.

PHET Simulation Documentary 3D Visualization
02

Reframing Knowledge · Shifting Paradigms

We correct the misconceptions that working engineers carry by habit. Voltage isn't just a flow — it's potential energy. Impedance isn't just resistance — it's a measure of how wave energy flows. This shift moves learners away from fragmented facts and toward first-principles thinking.

03

A True Bridge Between Theory and Field Practice

We draw a direct line from the chemical bonding, wave theory, and electromagnetics you learned in undergrad to how they actually show up in real CVD/Dry Etch equipment and RF matching networks. The analysis of real-time FDC parameters (Vrms, Irms, phase) carries 20+ years of the instructor's hands-on know-how.

04

Modular Design Tailored to the Learner

Class-1 and Class-2 are run selectively and flexibly depending on each learner's role (equipment operation, parts/materials development, etc.) and prior knowledge level. Difficulty is leveled for non-major and vocational-school students to prevent learning gaps.
