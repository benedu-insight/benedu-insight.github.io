RF CONTEXT

Electric Flux in Plasma & RF Systems

General physics vs. semiconductor/RF application perspective

Electric flux (Φ) measures how many electric field lines pass through a surface. The angle θ between the E-field direction and the surface normal determines how much field "penetrates" the surface. When θ = 0° the surface is perpendicular to E → maximum flux. When θ = 90° the surface is parallel to E → zero flux. The unit Nm²/C is equivalent to V·m, emphasizing its role as a "field × area" quantity.
In a CCP (Capacitively Coupled Plasma) chamber, the sheath electric field is nearly perpendicular to the electrode surface (θ ≈ 0°), maximizing ion acceleration flux toward the wafer — this is precisely why CCP etching produces directional (anisotropic) etch profiles. In ICP (Inductively Coupled Plasma) chambers, the RF coil generates a time-varying magnetic flux (Faraday's law), but the resulting E-field lines run parallel to the substrate → near-zero electric flux through the wafer → much lower ion bombardment energy, suitable for gentle deposition (e.g. ALD).

RF CONTEXT

Gauss's Law in Plasma Chambers

Why enclosed charge is the key concept in sheath physics

Gauss's law states that the total electric flux through any closed surface equals the total enclosed charge divided by ε₀. The shape or size of the Gaussian surface is irrelevant — only the enclosed charge Qenc matters. A neutral object (Qenc = 0) produces zero net flux regardless of the external field, which explains electric shielding.
In a plasma chamber, the plasma sheath is a region of net positive charge (ion-rich space-charge layer) adjacent to each electrode. By Gauss's law, this uncompensated positive charge creates a strong inward-pointing E-field inside the sheath — the sheath electric field — which accelerates ions toward the electrode (wafer). The total sheath voltage (Vsheath) scales with the enclosed space-charge density. In RF systems, the RF Generator delivers power that sustains this charge imbalance cycle-by-cycle, directly controlling ion bombardment energy on the wafer.

RF CONTEXT

Symmetry Choice in Real RF Equipment

Which geometry governs your plasma reactor?

The choice of Gaussian surface reflects the physical symmetry of the charge distribution. Spherical: point charges, isolated conductors → E ∝ 1/r². Cylindrical: long wire, coaxial cable → E ∝ 1/r. Planar: large flat conductor → E = constant. Each geometry is selected so that E is constant and parallel to dA on the Gaussian surface, making the surface integral trivial.
CCP (Parallel Plate): The electrode geometry is inherently planar → E = σ/ε₀ inside the sheath. The DC self-bias voltage (VDC) on the RF-driven electrode is governed by this planar E-field integrated across the sheath thickness.

ICP (Toroidal): The RF coil has cylindrical/toroidal geometry → the induced E-field follows cylindrical symmetry inside the plasma (E ∝ 1/r from the coil axis).

RF Generator & Coaxial Cable: The 50Ω transmission line between generator and chamber is a perfect cylindrical geometry — inner conductor and outer shield separated by dielectric → E ∝ 1/r, which is why coaxial cables have logarithmic capacitance per unit length: C/L = 2πε/ln(b/a).
