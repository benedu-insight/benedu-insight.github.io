INSIGHT

The gate never sees anything but a full-swing on/off command (Zone 2), so there is no drive level to turn down the way a linear amp would. Instead, C1 and omega are fixed by the resonant design, which fixes Idc/Vdd, so the only knob left is Vdd itself, and P = Vdd times Idc turns that into a squared relationship. This is why Zone 5's power supply has to be variable, not fixed, for a switching-family generator.

DEVICE RATING CHECK

Calibrated to a real device: the IXYS DE375-102N12A used in this hardware is rated VDSS = 1000V. At this rail, peak switch voltage reaches about 641V, within the recommended 750V (75% of VDSS) reliability margin. This value updates live with the Vdd slider above.

INSIGHT

Derived directly from the two ZVS boundary conditions (v=0 and dv/dt=0 at turn-on), not just quoted: with 50% duty, I_R/I_dc is about 1.862 and phi is about -32.5 degrees. Notice how small the tuning error is on the x-axis but how much turn-on voltage it produces. Class E is inherently more sensitive to component tolerance and load variation than a linear (Class AB) amplifier, which is exactly why some manufacturers still choose linear for reflection-heavy loads.

Once shaped by this resonant network, the near-fundamental waveform moves on to Zone 4's Matching Network for 50 ohm impedance transformation, a separate topic covered by the PRISM matching simulator.
