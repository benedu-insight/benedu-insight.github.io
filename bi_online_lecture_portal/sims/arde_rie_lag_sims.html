INSIGHT

Because etch rate falls as aspect ratio rises, the narrowest opening is always the bottleneck. Process time is dictated by that slowest feature; every wider feature receives extra etch time. That forced overetch can open the stop layer, create footing/notching, or degrade CD control in open areas and large contacts — exactly why “the slowest feature sets your overetch.”

Depth vs time — how lag accumulates

Each curve is a different trench width. Vertical line = moment the narrowest hits target.

Solid curves = depth history. Dashed vertical = time when the narrowest feature reaches the target. Horizontal dashed = target depth. The gap above the target on the wide-feature curves is the overetch you are forced to accept.

INSIGHT

Early in the etch all features advance at nearly the same rate (low AR). As the narrow trench deepens, its AR climbs faster, its rate drops sooner, and the depth gap widens. Raising β (stronger ARDE) or targeting deeper etches both amplify the final overetch on the wide features. Reducing β (process tuning, higher ion energy, chemistry changes) is the only way to shrink the forced overetch without sacrificing the narrowest CD.

The design must clear the slowest (narrowest) feature. Everything wider is already overetched by the amount shown above. That extra depth budget is what your stop-layer selectivity and CD control must absorb.
