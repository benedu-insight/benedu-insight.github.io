V, I AT THE SENSOR
V = Vfwd(1+Gamma) ; I = Vfwd(1-Gamma)/Z0
INSIGHT

This is exactly why a coherent V/I sensor board mixes V and I with the same LO (split by a transformer to both channels): if the two channels didn't share a local oscillator, the relative phase between V and I would be scrambled by the downconversion itself, and this whole angle -- the only place the load's reactance shows up -- would be lost.

PATH A
Vfwd=(V+I*Z0)/2 ; Vrefl=(V-I*Z0)/2 ; P=|V|^2/Z0
PATH B
Z = V / I
INSIGHT

Same two numbers, V and I, feed both paths. The RF Generator's directional coupler is really just Path A hard-wired in analog form (the coupler's physical geometry does the add/subtract). The Matcher's V/I sensor keeps V and I separate all the way to the FPGA and lets digital math pick whichever path -- or both -- it needs. Neither sensor is "better" -- they're built for different questions: how much power is flowing (Generator) vs. what does the load look like (Matcher).
