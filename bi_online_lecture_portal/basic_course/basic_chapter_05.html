Both are measured in joules: 1 J = 1 N × 1 m. Count W as work done BY the body: its energy changes by ΔE = −W.둘 다 단위는 줄(J)입니다. 1 J = 1 N × 1 m. W를 물체가 한 일로 세면, 에너지 변화는 ΔE = −W입니다.

Energy is what a body has; work is what it does. A 2 kg block lifted 1 m holds 2 × 9.8 × 1 ≈ 19.6 J of potential energy. That energy is a promise: it can do 19.6 J of work in the future. When we let it fall and turn a generator, the promise is cashed in: the block does 19.6 J of work and its stored energy drops by exactly 19.6 J.

Because work is energy on the move, both use the same unit, the joule (1 J = 1 newton × 1 metre). The sign tells the direction, so we must pick one point of view. Here W is the work done BY the block. While it is lifted, the block is being pushed against its own weight: it does −19.6 J of work and its energy rises by +19.6 J. When it falls and drives the generator, it does +19.6 J of work and its energy drops by 19.6 J. Either way ΔE = −W. (Seen from the motor’s side, the motor did +19.6 J of work ON the block: the same amount with the opposite sign.) Press Lift and Release and watch the two bars mirror each other.

For a sense of scale: lifting an apple (about 100 g) by 1 m takes about 1 J. One electron-volt from Chapter 3 is only 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, a tiny amount, which is why we use eV for single atoms and electrons.

에너지는 물체가 가지고 있는 것이고, 일은 물체가 하는 것입니다. 2 kg 블록을 1 m 들어 올리면 2 × 9.8 × 1 ≈ 19.6 J의 위치 에너지를 가집니다. 이 에너지는 일종의 약속입니다. 앞으로 19.6 J의 일을 할 수 있다는 뜻입니다. 블록을 떨어뜨려 발전기를 돌리면 약속이 실행되어, 블록이 19.6 J의 일을 하고 저장된 에너지는 정확히 19.6 J 줄어듭니다.

일은 옮겨 가는 에너지이므로 둘은 같은 단위인 줄(J, 1 J = 1 N × 1 m)을 씁니다. 부호는 방향을 나타내므로 기준을 하나로 정해야 합니다. 여기서 W는 블록이 한 일입니다. 들어 올릴 때 블록은 자기 무게를 거슬러 밀려 올라가므로 −19.6 J의 일을 하고, 에너지는 +19.6 J 늘어납니다. 떨어지며 발전기를 돌릴 때는 +19.6 J의 일을 하고, 에너지는 19.6 J 줄어듭니다. 어느 쪽이든 ΔE = −W입니다. (모터 쪽에서 보면 모터가 블록에 +19.6 J의 일을 해 준 것이고, 같은 양에 부호만 반대입니다.) 들어 올리기와 놓기를 눌러 두 막대가 거울처럼 움직이는 것을 보세요.

크기를 느껴 봅시다. 사과 한 개(약 100 g)를 1 m 들어 올리는 데 약 1 J이 듭니다. 3장의 1 eV는 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J에 불과한 아주 작은 양이라서, 원자와 전자 하나를 다룰 때는 eV를 씁니다.

All of these are the same quantity, measured in joules. Electromagnetic energy is carried by electric and magnetic fields and by light. Kinetic energy belongs to anything that moves. Thermal energy is the random motion of atoms that we feel as temperature. Potential energy is stored by position: a raised block, a stretched spring, or a charge sitting in an electric field. Chemical energy is stored in bonds, nuclear energy in nuclei.

The law of conservation of energy says the total never changes; energy only moves from one form to another. Press "Essential in semiconductors" to see the three forms a plasma tool cannot work without: electromagnetic, kinetic and thermal. The next slide looks at each one.

Electromagnetic

The RF Generator delivers electromagnetic energy, usually at 13.56 MHz and a few kilowatts. The oscillating electric field pushes the light electrons back and forth, and they pick up energy from it. This is the only door through which energy enters the plasma. The plasma also gives some back as light, which we read with OES.

Kinetic

The electrons’ kinetic energy does the chemistry: an electron with 3 eV moves at about 1,000 km/s and can excite, dissociate or ionize molecules. Near the wafer, ions are accelerated by the sheath (Chapter 6) and arrive with tens to hundreds of eV. An Ar⁺ ion with 100 eV moves at about 22 km/s. That impact energy is what makes etching directional (anisotropic) and what knocks atoms out of a PVD target.

Thermal

The wafer sits on a chuck that heats or cools it, often with helium gas on the back side for good heat contact. Temperature sets the reaction rate: PE-CVD runs hot (≈ 300–400 °C) so films grow well; many Dry Etch steps run near room temperature or below. Heat is also where most of the input power finally ends up, so it must be removed.

Energy is never created or lost. It changes form.에너지는 만들어지거나 사라지지 않고 형태만 바뀝니다.

Power is how fast energy flows: 1 watt = 1 joule per second. That is why RF Generators are rated in watts or kilowatts, not joules. A 2 kW generator running for one minute delivers 120,000 J (120 kJ). In a real tool most of that energy finally ends up as heat in the Chamber walls and the wafer chuck, which is why both are cooled.

A plasma tool is an energy converter. Electrical power goes into the electrons, the electrons transfer it in collisions, and the plasma outputs light, reactive radicals, energetic ions and heat. Everything we learned in chapters 3 and 4 happens at stage 3. This chapter explains stages 1 and 2: how a field gives energy to an electron.

1700s – 1800s

Units came before the electron전자보다 단위가 먼저

Electricity was studied as visible effects: static sparks, battery currents, magnetic forces between wires. The electron itself was discovered by J.J. Thomson only in 1897. The units were set before anyone knew it existed.전기는 18~19세기에 정전기, 배터리 전류, 전선 사이의 자기력처럼 눈에 보이는 현상으로 먼저 연구되었습니다. 전류를 만드는 알갱이인 전자는 1897년에야 J.J. 톰슨이 발견했습니다. 전자가 있는지조차 모르는 상태에서 단위가 먼저 만들어진 것입니다.

1881 · PARIS

Defined by current × time전류 × 시간으로 정의

The International Congress of Electricians in Paris adopted the coulomb, named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb. Nobody could count charge directly, so 1 C was defined as the charge moved by 1 A in 1 s. The ampere itself came from the magnetic force produced by currents (from 1948, the force between two parallel wires).1881년 파리 국제 전기 회의에서 쿨롱의 법칙을 발견한 샤를 오귀스탱 드 쿨롱의 이름을 딴 단위 "쿨롱"을 공식 채택했습니다. 전하를 직접 셀 수 없었으므로 "1 A의 전류가 1초 동안 흐를 때 이동하는 전기량"을 1 C으로 정했습니다. 이때 1 A는 전류가 만드는 자기력을 기준으로 정한 값입니다(1948년부터는 두 평행 전선 사이의 힘으로 정의).

2019 · SI

Now the other way round이제는 거꾸로

Since the 2019 SI redefinition, the elementary charge is fixed exactly at e = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, and the coulomb and ampere are defined from it. Nature’s unit now anchors the human one.2019년 SI 재정의 이후로는 기본 전하를 e = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C으로 정확히 고정하고, 거꾸로 이 값에서 쿨롱과 암페어를 정의합니다. 이제는 자연의 단위가 인간의 단위를 붙잡아 주는 셈입니다.

Why not just use one electron as 1? Engineers of the telegraph and power age needed practical numbers. Even 1 A moves about 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons every second. With the electron as the unit, everyday currents would be written with absurdly large numbers. A charge changes the space around it. Any other charge placed there feels a force, even without touching. That region of influence is the electric field. To measure it, imagine a tiny positive test charge q: the field at that point is the force on it divided by its charge, E = F / q. Its unit is newtons per coulomb (N/C), which is the same as volts per metre (V/m).

The field has a size and a direction. Its direction is the way a positive charge would be pushed: away from a + charge, toward a − charge. A negative charge such as an electron feels a force opposite to the arrows. Around a single point charge the field falls with distance, E = kQ / r², the same inverse-square law as Coulomb’s law.

Field lines are a picture of the field: they start on + charges and end on − charges, they never cross, and where they are crowded the field is strong. In the lab, drag the test charge around and watch the arrow and the numbers, then release it to see it follow the field. The electric field E is the force per unit charge at a point. Between two parallel plates the field is uniform, and E = V / d (volts divided by distance). A bigger voltage or a smaller gap gives a stronger field. The field points from + to −; an electron, being negative, is pushed the other way, toward the + plate, with force F = eE.

Units: the SI unit of E is V/m (the same as N/C used on the previous slide). Plasma and gas-discharge work usually quotes V/cm because electrode gaps are a few centimetres; 1 V/cm = 100 V/m. For example, air breaks down at about 30 kV/cm.

An electron that crosses the whole gap gains energy eV: with 300 V it picks up 300 eV, far more than the 15.8 eV needed to ionize argon. In a real plasma it collides long before that, so it usually carries only a few eV. A positive ion such as Ar⁺ feels the same size of force in the opposite direction, but it is about 73,000 times heavier than an electron, so it speeds up far more slowly.

For a large flat plate with surface charge density σ, Gauss's law gives E = σ / ε₀, where ε₀ ≈ 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m is the permittivity of free space. This is the same E = V/d seen from the charge side. This is the geometry of a CCP: two parallel electrodes with a gas between them.

Michael Faraday (1791–1867) found in 1831 that a changing magnetic field makes a voltage in a nearby coil. A magnet that sits still does nothing; only change matters. The faster the change, the larger the voltage: ε = −dΦ/dt, where Φ is the magnetic flux through the coil. The minus sign (Lenz's law) says the induced current opposes the change.

Drag the magnet into the coil and out again, or press Auto. Watch the graph: the voltage (yellow) is the slope of the flux (purple). It is largest where the flux changes fastest, zero at the top of the flux curve, and flips sign when the magnet turns back. More turns N or faster motion give a bigger voltage. The small N or S on the coil shows Lenz’s law: the coil always makes a pole that fights the motion.

Move the magnet back and forth: the meter swings when the magnet moves and returns to zero when it stops. This is exactly how an ICP works. A coil carries RF current, so the magnetic field changes thousands of times per microsecond, which induces a circulating electric field in the gas that heats the electrons without any electrode touching the plasma.

In 1820 Hans Christian Ørsted noticed that a compass needle jumped when a current flowed in a nearby wire: electric current makes a magnetic field. André-Marie Ampère quickly turned this into a law. Around a long straight wire the field lines are circles, and the field gets weaker with distance: B = μ₀I / 2πr, where μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A. 10 A at 2 cm gives 100 µT, about twice the Earth’s field.

Biot–Savart law. Just weeks after Ørsted’s report in 1820, Jean-Baptiste Biot and Félix Savart measured how the field of a current-carrying wire falls off with distance. Their law gives the field made by each tiny piece of wire: dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl × r̂ / r². Add up the pieces of a long straight wire and you get exactly B = μ₀I / 2πr, the same answer as Ampère’s law. Biot–Savart works for any shape of wire, piece by piece; Ampère’s law is the shortcut when the shape is symmetric.

Why the letter B? It is often said to stand for Biot–Savart, but there is no historical record of that. The letter comes from James Clerk Maxwell’s Treatise (1873), where he simply labelled his vectors in order: A (vector potential), B (magnetic induction), C (current), D (displacement), E (electric field), and so on to H. B was just the next letter after A.

Direction comes from the right-hand rule: point your thumb along the current, and your fingers curl the way B goes. In the lab, the wire carries current out of the page (⊙) or into it (⊗). Drag the probe to see B at any distance, and watch the compass needles line up along the circles. Ampère’s law says it in general form: add up B along any closed loop (∮B·dl) and you get μ₀ times the current passing through it.

Switch to AC (RF): the current reverses again and again, so the field around it reverses too and the compasses flip back and forth. In an ICP antenna this happens 13.56 million times a second. That rapidly changing B is exactly what Faraday’s law (previous slide) turns into an electric field inside the plasma. Winding the wire into a coil adds the fields of all turns together, which is why antennas and electromagnets are coils.

1820년 한스 크리스티안 외르스테드는 근처 도선에 전류가 흐를 때 나침반 바늘이 움직이는 것을 발견했습니다. 전류가 자기장을 만든다는 뜻입니다. 앙드레 마리 앙페르(암페어)는 곧 이것을 법칙으로 정리했습니다. 긴 직선 도선 주위의 자기력선은 동심원이고, 거리가 멀어질수록 약해집니다. B = μ₀I / 2πr이며 μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A입니다. 10 A가 흐르는 도선에서 2 cm 떨어진 곳은 100 µT로, 지구 자기장의 약 2배입니다.

비오-사바르 법칙. 1820년 외르스테드의 발표 후 불과 몇 주 만에 장바티스트 비오와 펠릭스 사바르는 전류가 흐르는 도선의 자기장이 거리에 따라 어떻게 약해지는지 측정했습니다. 이들의 법칙은 도선의 아주 작은 조각 하나가 만드는 자기장을 알려 줍니다. dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl × r̂ / r². 긴 직선 도선의 조각들을 모두 더하면 정확히 B = μ₀I / 2πr로, 암페어 법칙과 같은 답이 나옵니다. 비오-사바르 법칙은 어떤 모양의 도선이든 조각조각 더해서 구할 수 있고, 암페어 법칙은 모양이 대칭일 때 쓰는 지름길입니다.

자기장을 왜 B로 쓸까요? 흔히 비오-사바르(Biot–Savart)의 머리글자라고 알려져 있지만, 이를 뒷받침하는 역사적 기록은 없습니다. 실제로는 제임스 클러크 맥스웰이 1873년 저서 『전기와 자기에 관한 논문(Treatise)』에서 벡터에 알파벳 순서대로 기호를 붙인 데서 왔습니다. A(벡터 퍼텐셜), B(자기 유도), C(전류), D(전기 변위), E(전기장) … H까지 이어지며, B는 단지 A 다음 글자였습니다.

방향은 오른손 법칙으로 정합니다. 엄지를 전류 방향으로 두면 나머지 네 손가락이 감기는 방향이 B의 방향입니다. 실습에서 도선의 전류는 화면 밖으로(⊙) 또는 화면 안으로(⊗) 흐릅니다. 탐침을 끌어 거리마다 B를 확인하고, 나침반 바늘이 원을 따라 정렬되는 모습을 보세요. 암페어 법칙은 이것을 일반적으로 표현합니다. 닫힌 경로를 따라 B를 모두 더하면(∮B·dl) 그 경로를 통과하는 전류의 μ₀배가 됩니다.

교류 AC (RF)로 바꿔 보세요. 전류의 방향이 계속 바뀌므로 주변 자기장도 바뀌고 나침반이 앞뒤로 뒤집힙니다. ICP 안테나에서는 이것이 1초에 1,356만 번 일어납니다. 이렇게 빠르게 변하는 B를 앞 장의 패러데이 법칙이 플라즈마 속 전기장으로 바꿉니다. 도선을 코일로 감으면 각 바퀴의 자기장이 더해지므로 안테나와 전자석은 코일 모양입니다.

Four equations describe all of classical electricity and magnetism, from a spark to a radio wave to light. You do not need to solve them; read them as sentences. 1) Charges make electric fields. 2) Magnetic field lines are closed loops: there are no magnetic charges. 3) A changing B makes a circulating E (Faraday; note the minus sign). 4) A current, or a changing E, makes a circulating B. Maxwell added the last term in 4), which is the key to waves. Click any card to study that equation in detail.

Each law can be written two ways. The differential form (∇) describes what happens at a single point: ∇· (divergence) measures how much a field spreads out of a point, ∇× (curl) how much it circulates around it. The integral form (∮) describes a whole closed surface or loop, and is usually the easier one to picture. They say exactly the same thing. Use the toggle to switch.

History: Maxwell’s original 1865 paper had twenty equations in twenty unknowns. In 1884 Oliver Heaviside rewrote them with vectors into the four we use today. Symbols: ρ charge density, J current density, ε₀ permittivity and μ₀ permeability of free space. Equation 1 describes the field between CCP electrodes, equation 2 the magnet arches of a magnetron, equation 3 powers ICP, and equations 3 and 4 together make light.

One photon, one electron광자 하나, 전자 하나

Light arrives in packets (photons) with E = hf. One photon gives all its energy to one electron. If E is bigger than the work function W, the electron escapes with E − W left over. Brightness only changes how many photons arrive. This won Einstein the 1921 Nobel Prize.빛은 E = hf의 에너지를 가진 알갱이(광자)로 옵니다. 광자 하나가 전자 하나에게 에너지를 모두 줍니다. E가 일함수 W보다 크면 전자는 E − W의 에너지를 가지고 튀어나옵니다. 밝기는 도착하는 광자의 수만 바꿉니다. 아인슈타인은 이 연구로 1921년 노벨상을 받았습니다.

WAVE AND PARTICLE

Both are true둘 다 맞다

Hertz showed light travels as a wave; Einstein showed it is absorbed as particles. Light is both: it spreads like a wave and hands over energy one photon at a time. Millikan confirmed E = hf − W precisely in 1916.헤르츠는 빛이 파동으로 퍼진다는 것을, 아인슈타인은 빛이 알갱이로 흡수된다는 것을 보였습니다. 빛은 둘 다입니다. 파동처럼 퍼지고, 에너지는 광자 하나씩 건넵니다. 1916년 밀리컨이 E = hf − W를 정밀하게 확인했습니다.

A twist of history. Hertz himself first noticed it in 1887: ultraviolet light made his receiver sparks jump more easily. The same experiment that proved light is a wave held the first clue that it is also a particle. In the fab: UV photons from a plasma knock electrons out of Chamber walls and wafers, and the same 1240/λ rule tells you which light can do it.역사의 반전. 이 현상을 처음 본 사람은 1887년의 헤르츠였습니다. 자외선을 비추면 수신기의 불꽃이 더 잘 튀었습니다. 빛이 파동임을 증명한 바로 그 실험에, 빛이 알갱이라는 첫 단서가 숨어 있었습니다. 장비에서는: 플라즈마의 자외선 광자가 Chamber 벽과 웨이퍼에서 전자를 떼어 냅니다. 어떤 빛이 그럴 수 있는지는 같은 1240/λ 규칙으로 알 수 있습니다.

Maxwell's equations 3 and 4 feed each other: a changing E makes a B, which changes and makes an E, and so on. The disturbance travels away at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s. That is an electromagnetic wave, and light is one. Radio waves, microwaves, visible light and X-rays differ only in frequency.

Move the slider. Frequency and wavelength are tied by c = fλ: our RF at 13.56 MHz has a wavelength of about 22 m, and a 2.45 GHz microwave about 12 cm. Photon energy E = hf grows with frequency: RF photons carry about 6×10⁻⁸ eV, far too little to ionize anything by themselves, while a UV photon carries several eV. RF power ionizes by accelerating electrons in the field, not by single photons. The color bands here are approximate.

A handy shortcut: E = hf = hc/λ, and hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm, so E(eV) ≈ 1240 / λ(nm). Red light at 620 nm carries 2 eV; ArF lithography light at 193 nm carries 6.4 eV, enough to break bonds in the photoresist; EUV at 13.5 nm carries 92 eV. The same formula lets you read an OES line: 656 nm means electrons fell across a 1.89 eV gap.

맥스웰 방정식의 3번과 4번은 서로를 키웁니다. 변하는 E가 B를 만들고, 그 B가 변해 다시 E를 만들며 이어집니다. 이 교란이 c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s로 퍼져 나갑니다. 이것이 전자기파이고 빛도 그 하나입니다. 라디오파, 마이크로파, 가시광선, X선은 주파수만 다릅니다.

슬라이더를 움직여 보세요. 주파수와 파장은 c = fλ로 묶여 있습니다. 13.56 MHz의 RF는 파장이 약 22 m, 2.45 GHz 마이크로파는 약 12 cm입니다. 광자 에너지 E = hf는 주파수에 비례합니다. RF 광자는 약 6×10⁻⁸ eV로 혼자서는 아무것도 이온화할 수 없고, 자외선 광자는 수 eV입니다. RF 전력은 광자 하나하나가 아니라 전기장 속에서 전자를 가속해 이온화합니다. 색 구간은 근사값입니다.

편리한 공식: E = hf = hc/λ이고 hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm이므로 E(eV) ≈ 1240 / λ(nm)입니다. 620 nm 빨간빛은 2 eV, 193 nm ArF 노광 빛은 6.4 eV로 감광막의 결합을 끊을 수 있고, 13.5 nm EUV는 92 eV입니다. OES 선을 해석할 때도 같은 식을 씁니다. 656 nm 선은 전자가 1.89 eV 간격을 떨어졌다는 뜻입니다.
